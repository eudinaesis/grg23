Concepts in two languages
EnglishDeutschExplanationExample
PolycrisisPolykriseA situation in which several crises interact so that their connections may create greater danger or difficulty than considering each crisis alone. The term is a question to investigate, not a label that proves the connections exist.A class tests whether an energy shock, higher costs and household pressure form connected mechanisms in a particular case.
CrisisKriseA serious disruption or turning point that puts important systems, people or institutions under pressure. The word needs a stated time, place and perspective.A sudden interruption to an essential supply can create a crisis for users who have few alternatives.
Causal mechanismWirkungsmechanismusThe steps that explain how one event or condition could produce a change in another. A mechanism needs evidence and may have limits.A shortage raises costs for a producer, which may then raise the price charged to customers.
Causal chainWirkungsketteA sequence of connected steps in which one change contributes to the next.A disruption affects supply; reduced supply changes costs; changed costs may affect household bills.
Feedback loopRückkopplungA sequence in which an effect feeds back and changes the process that helped cause it. A loop can strengthen or weaken a change.A price increase may reduce demand, which can ease pressure on supply; whether this happens depends on available alternatives.
Interdependencegegenseitige AbhängigkeitA relationship in which people, organisations or countries rely on one another for goods, services, money, rules or infrastructure.A factory may depend on imported materials while its suppliers depend on orders from that factory.
ExposureBetroffenheit / ExpositionThe extent to which a person, group or system is in contact with a possible harm or disruption. Exposure does not by itself say how severe the effect will be.Households using one heating source may be more exposed to a disruption in that source.
VulnerabilityVerwundbarkeitHow likely a person or system is to suffer harm when exposed, shaped by resources, alternatives, protection and ability to recover.Two households face the same price rise, but one has less income and fewer ways to reduce energy use.
ResilienceWiderstandsfähigkeitThe capacity to withstand disruption, adapt, recover or change. Ask whose resilience is being discussed and who pays for it.More than one source of supply may help a service continue during a disruption.
Distributional effectVerteilungswirkungThe way a policy or change distributes benefits, costs, risks or opportunities across different groups.A broad subsidy may help many users, while giving larger cash benefits to those who use more energy.
Externalityexterne WirkungA cost or benefit of an activity that affects others and is not fully reflected in the decision-maker’s price or choice.Pollution from production can affect nearby people who are not part of the purchase.
InflationInflationA broad rise in the general level of prices across goods and services, measured over a stated period. It is different from one item becoming more expensive, and from the price level remaining high after inflation slows.If inflation falls, prices may still be rising, but more slowly than in the previous period.
Foreign exchangeDevisenMoney held or traded in another currency, used for payments such as imports or debts owed in that currency.A company may need foreign currency to pay an overseas supplier.
Exchange rateWechselkursThe price of one currency in terms of another. A change can alter the local cost of imports and foreign-currency debts.If a country’s currency loses value against the currency used for an import, the import may cost more in local money.
Public debtStaatsschuldenMoney a government owes after borrowing. Its effects depend on terms such as interest, currency, maturity, creditors and the government’s capacity to repay.A government with large repayments due soon may have less room in its budget for other spending.
Policy spacepolitischer HandlungsspielraumThe practical room a government has to choose and carry out policies, given its resources, laws, institutions, economic conditions and external commitments.A government may want to support households but face a limited budget and rules about how it can spend.
Energy transitionEnergiewende / Übergang zu einem klimafreundlicheren EnergiesystemChanges in how energy is produced, moved and used, including changes to infrastructure, technology, work and rules. It involves choices and trade-offs.Replacing a heating system can change emissions, household costs and demand for skilled workers.
Supply chainLieferketteThe linked activities and organisations that provide materials, make a product and deliver it to users.A battery supply chain can include mining, refining, component production, assembly and transport.
EvidenceBeleg / EvidenzInformation that supports, weakens or helps assess a claim. Evidence can include data, documents, observations or testimony; its quality and limits must be considered.A price chart may show when prices changed, but it cannot by itself prove why they changed.
Independent evidenceunabhängiger BelegEvidence gathered or produced through a source that is not simply repeating the same claim or relying on the same original source. Independence is a matter to check, not assume.A writer’s claim about prices can be checked against a separately produced statistical series.
Trade-offZielkonflikt / AbwägungA situation where improving one goal may make another goal harder to achieve, so choices and priorities must be explained.A quick price limit may give immediate relief but could have different effects on public spending or incentives.
Alternative explanationalternative ErklärungAnother plausible account of why an event happened or a pattern appeared. Comparing alternatives helps test a causal claim.If bills rose, a project should consider more than one possible cause, such as supply costs and policy choices.

Teacher-written working definitions. Historical claims and numerical evidence need the source trail in each lesson.
