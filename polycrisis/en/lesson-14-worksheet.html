Project workshop: make the question smaller

How can we turn a large crisis topic into a researchable question?

Your 100-minute lesson

  1. 10 min · Choose a route

    Bring forward the focused question and two starting sources from lesson 13 homework. Read ‘Project route cards’ and individually confirm or refine your route. Complete topic, place/group, time, and affected group on ‘Question narrowing ladder’. Deliverable: a specific case linked to two sources.

  2. 15 min · Read the project reader

    Read ‘Project reader: from topic to investigation’. Annotate the model question for scope, mechanism, evidence, and uncertainty. Deliverable: four annotations.

  3. 15 min · Draft and test questions

    Use ‘Question test cards’ with a partner. Draft two questions; test each against scope, evidence access, and fairness to competing explanations. Deliverable: one revised research question.

  4. 5 min · Break

    Take a five-minute break.

  5. 20 min · Source clinic

    Use ‘Source comparison cards’ and ‘Source log template’. Classify each item as data, analysis, or advocacy; record what it can and cannot establish. Deliverable: three source-log entries, including one source you still need.

  6. 20 min · Build the investigation plan

    Use ‘Project planner worksheet’ and the course project rubric/planner. Make a preliminary causal map; select two possible responses; sketch the 2–3 charts you will need, including their source and unit. Deliverable: completed one-page plan.

  7. 10 min · Teacher conference and peer check

    Exchange plans using ‘Project checkpoint card’. Ask whether the question is answerable and whether each arrow has a plausible evidence source. Deliverable: one revision and one unresolved question.

  8. 5 min · Exit: next research step

    Use ‘Exit and homework slip’ to state your next small research action and one risk to your explanation. Deliverable: individual exit response.

Materials

Project reader: from topic to investigation

Why narrow the question?

A topic such as ‘climate change and the economy’ is important but too wide for one short investigation. A research question names a place or group, a period, a mechanism, and something we can investigate with evidence. For example: ‘How did Sri Lanka’s loss of foreign currency from tourism during the COVID-19 period interact with its ability to pay for imports, and what do official data show about the timing?’ This is still a large question, but it has a place, time, mechanism, and evidence trail. It does not assume that tourism alone caused the country’s crisis.

Use different kinds of sources for different jobs. A statistical agency may provide a series; a central bank may explain a policy problem from its institutional viewpoint; a research paper may test an explanation; a news report may show how an event was experienced or contested. None is automatically neutral or useless. Ask who produced the source, for what audience, with what method, and what it can establish. Check dates and units before combining figures. A line chart of monthly currency reserves and a chart of annual tourism income do not have the same time scale.

Your investigation should show how a mechanism could work, then test it against evidence and alternative explanations. A causal map is a set of hypotheses, not proof. Mark missing links and uncertainty. Compare at least two responses, including who pays, who benefits, who has authority, and likely side effects. You can conclude that evidence is mixed or the polycrisis label adds little. Your job is not to defend a predetermined answer. The course project rubric/planner gives the final length, source, chart, and oral defence requirements.

Project route cards

Choose one route; all are starting points, not pre-approved conclusions

  • Energy and households: Under what conditions can building renovation reduce both energy vulnerability and household costs in Austria? Find out what evidence is available and define one group or programme.
  • Debt and disaster: How might foreign-currency debt limit a government's response after a disaster? Use one country and one dated event; distinguish a mechanism from proof of causation.
  • Batteries and supply chains: Can battery manufacturing advance climate goals while creating new dependencies? Choose one link in a supply chain and identify who holds bargaining power.
  • Energy shock comparison: What does the 1970s comparison illuminate—and obscure—about Europe's 2021–23 energy crisis?
  • Student-proposed: Identify a place, dates, at least two interacting systems, and an evidence source before asking the teacher to approve the question.

Source shortlist and fallback investigation

Start with these real, public sources

  • Sri Lanka route: Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority arrivals reports (official counts); IMF 2023 programme report (institutional account of shocks, policies, reserves and reforms); World Bank Sri Lanka Development Update 2023 (analysis of structural weaknesses and recovery risks). Read each source as a perspective, not a final verdict.
  • Battery route: IEA Batteries and Secure Energy Transitions (2024), section Status of battery demand and supply; IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2024 (mineral supply risks and policies); add an independent analysis and a source for workers or industry in a chosen country.
  • Austria energy route: Statistik Austria VPI/HVPI (dated price measures); OeNB teaching materials for background; find one specific renovation programme or public evaluation and one independent analysis.
  • 1970s comparison: use lesson 13 evidence cards plus IEA 2022 Energy Crisis summary; add an independent historical analysis.

Fallback question using supplied sources

If other sources are inaccessible or your question is not yet feasible, investigate: What do Sri Lanka tourism and crisis sources show about how an external shock may interact with limited foreign exchange, and what can they not prove? Supplied evidence: SLTDA reports 1,913,702 tourist arrivals in 2019 and 507,704 in 2020 (arrivals, not tourism revenue); the IMF 2023 report describes a series of shocks and policy missteps and reports depleted reserves before debt service was suspended in spring 2022; the World Bank's 2023 update describes longstanding weaknesses elevated by several shocks and reports usable reserves of US$2.4 billion at end-July 2023, equivalent to 5–6 weeks of goods-and-services imports. Do not claim falling arrivals alone caused the crisis. These items support a source comparison and plan; a final project still needs the common source and chart requirements.

Distinguish tourist arrivals from foreign-currency earnings and institutional interpretation from directly measured data. Check dates and definitions.

Question test cards

  • Scope test: Could this be answered in 1,000–1,200 words?
  • Mechanism test: Does the question ask how something works, not merely whether a broad topic is ‘good’ or ‘bad’?
  • Evidence test: Can I find at least four sources, including independent evidence and a contrasting analysis, as required by the course project rubric/planner?
  • Chart test: Can I identify 2–3 charts with dates, units, and accessible source data?
  • Alternative test: What else might explain the pattern?
  • Fairness test: Have I described affected groups and decision-makers without treating a country or population as one actor?
  • Privacy test: Can I investigate this without asking classmates to disclose personal finances, health, migration status, or family experience?

Project planner worksheet

  1. Working question: …
  2. Why this case and time period? …
  3. Draft answer (may change): …
  4. Mechanism map: A → [because…] B → [because…] C. Put a question mark where the link needs testing.
  5. Alternative explanation: …
  6. Two response options: For each, name likely beneficiaries, cost-bearers, decision-maker, and possible side effect.
  7. Chart 1–3: question answered; variables; unit; dates; source URL; one caveat.
  8. Sources still needed: data/evidence; independent analysis; contrasting analysis; institutional or affected-group perspective.
  9. What could make me revise my answer? …

Use this with the course project rubric/planner and source log. The final investigation requirements are 1,000–1,200 words, 2–3 charts, at least four sources including independent evidence and contrasting analysis, and an individual oral defence.

Project checkpoint card

  • Can I repeat your question in one sentence?
  • Is the place/group and period clear?
  • Which arrow in your map is least supported?
  • What source could check that arrow?
  • Have you included an alternative explanation?
  • Are the two policy options real alternatives with different costs or effects?

Feedback sentence: “Your plan is most researchable when …” / “Before drafting, check …”

Exit and homework slip

Exit

  1. What is your next research action, stated as a task you can finish in 20 minutes?
  2. What could weaken or change your current explanation?

Homework (20–25 minutes)

Draft one 200–300-word core paragraph answering your working question. Include an attributed source-based claim and one caveat. Add or adapt one chart with title, axes/units, date, source, and a one-sentence interpretation. Bring both to lesson 15. The final investigation is built in stages from this prework; it is not written from scratch in class.

Language bridge

English pathway. Use English for original sources where they add evidence or a distinct argument; do not choose a source only because it is in English. Start with the World Bank indicator database or a national statistics page, and inspect the indicator definition before copying a number. Austrian connection: use Statistik Austria’s VPI/HICP page for a dated price series and explain in English what its unit measures. Original-reading extension: on the World Bank WDI page, read the indicator title, definition, source, and metadata for one indicator you may use; write a 3-sentence English note distinguishing what it measures from what it does not measure. Your project can use German evidence, English analysis, or both.

Check: source genre, author, date, unit, method, perspective, and limitation. Ask the teacher if a proposed case requires sensitive personal information; choose an aggregate or public source instead.

What to hand in

Individual or pair one-page investigation plan, on paper or teacher assignment platform. It must contain a bounded question, preliminary mechanism map, one alternative explanation, two policy responses, planned 2–3 charts with units/dates/source leads, and a source log with three entries including one missing source. Use the course project rubric/planner.
