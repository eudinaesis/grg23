Student Chartbook conference

What can our investigations explain, and what remains uncertain?

Your 100-minute lesson

  1. 10 min · Set up and rehearse

    Use ‘Conference checklist’ to set up charts and source labels. In each group of four, the two project partners rehearse a two-minute presentation using ‘Presentation frame’. Deliverable: checked display and timed rehearsal.

  2. 15 min · Group-of-four project conference

    In six groups of four (normally two pairs), each pair gives one two-minute project presentation, then takes one minute for a question and response. Use ‘Audience evidence card’. Repeat for the second pair; use remaining time to complete peer notes. Deliverable: both projects heard and each presenter receives a question.

  3. 5 min · Break

    Take a five-minute break.

  4. 40 min · Single-teacher individual oral defences

    The teacher calls students one at a time for exactly 100 seconds each: two short answers of 40 seconds each, plus 20 seconds for the teacher to note evidence and transition. For 24 students, 24 × 100 seconds = 40 minutes. Use ‘Oral defence cards’; each student draws or is assigned two prepared questions. Everyone waiting completes the supplied ‘Independent chart and source audit’ using a project chart/source sheet from their group. Deliverable: 24 individual teacher-heard defences and one audit per student. If the class has more than 24 students, continue the remaining defences in the next lesson; do not add assumed staff.

  5. 15 min · Whole-class Chartbook synthesis

    Use ‘Conference synthesis board’. Add one finding, one disagreement or variation, one shared mechanism, and one unanswered question from the projects. Deliverable: shared synthesis with project titles/initials, not personal disclosures.

  6. 10 min · Individual course reflection

    Use ‘Final reflection form’ and the common course project rubric/planner. Revisit your first-week map and write what you would keep, redraw, or remove, with a reason. Deliverable: individual reflection.

  7. 5 min · Exit

    Answer the two questions on ‘Exit and follow-up’. Deliverable: individual response.

Materials

Presentation frame

Prepare a 2-minute pair presentation; divide the steps so both partners speak. Use this order:

  1. Question: I investigated … in/among … during …
  2. Why it matters: This matters because …
  3. Mechanism: One proposed connection is A → B because …
  4. Evidence: My strongest evidence is … (source/date/unit); chart … shows …
  5. Competing account or response: Another explanation/response is …
  6. Conclusion and limit: I conclude …, but I cannot establish …
  7. Polycrisis test: The concept helps/does not help here because …

Point to the axis titles and units when using a chart. Do not read every number aloud.

Conference checklist

  • Question is visible and bounded by place/group and time.
  • 2–3 charts have title, axes, units, dates, source, and a brief caveat.
  • At least four sources are listed, including independent evidence and a contrasting analysis, following the course project rubric/planner.
  • Any adapted chart labels or translated text are identified.
  • Names and personal information are omitted unless they are published, relevant, and necessary.
  • Presentation fits two minutes; every partner speaks and can answer about the project as a whole.

In the conference round, present as a pair for two minutes; each partner must speak. The oral defence is individual.

Audience evidence card

Presenter/project: …

  • Question and case I heard: …
  • Most convincing evidence (source/unit/date): …
  • Mechanism explained: …
  • One question I asked: …
  • What the project says it cannot establish: …
  • One idea I can use in another case: …

Ask about reasoning, evidence, definitions, or limits. Do not ask classmates to reveal household finances or personal/family experiences.

Oral defence cards

Each student answers two questions individually

  • What is the strongest evidence for your conclusion? Give its source and explain what it measures.
  • Which link in your explanation is most uncertain?
  • What is the strongest alternative explanation or contrasting analysis?
  • Who makes a key decision in this case, and what constrains that actor?
  • Who experiences the costs or benefits differently?
  • Does ‘polycrisis’ improve your explanation? Why or why not?
  • What evidence would make you change your conclusion?
  • Which response option did you compare, and what trade-off did you find?

Answer structure: direct answer → evidence → reasoning → limit. Students may keep a small source card with titles, units, and dates, but not a written speech.

Conference synthesis board

Build a shared chartbook from project findings
Bounded finding (project title/initials)Mechanism identifiedWho is affected differently?Uncertainty or disagreementPossible response / trade-off

Record conclusions as claims with scope, not universal laws. Different projects may disagree because they examine different periods, groups, data, or mechanisms.

Final reflection form

  1. My week-one map showed …
  2. I would now keep, redraw, or remove … because …
  3. A mechanism I can explain more clearly now is …
  4. A source or chart that changed or qualified my view was …
  5. The best challenge to my project was …; I responded by …
  6. For this case, ‘polycrisis’ is useful/not useful/partly useful because …
  7. One unanswered question I would investigate next is …

Use the common course project rubric/planner to check whether your final submission and individual oral defence are complete.

Exit and follow-up

Exit questions

  1. What is one claim you can now make more carefully than in week one?
  2. What is one question you would keep investigating after this course?

Follow-up task (15–20 minutes)

Make any final correction to a chart citation or source log after the conference. If the project is already complete, write a 100-word note explaining which audience question most improved your explanation.

Independent chart and source audit

Complete this individually while the teacher hears oral defences. Use one project chart and its source note from your conference group.

  1. Write the chart title and exact question it helps answer.
  2. Record x-axis, y-axis, units, dates, and source.
  3. Describe one pattern in one sentence without claiming a cause.
  4. What does the chart not measure or prove?
  5. Write one question for the project author about the source or interpretation.
  6. Check the source note: who produced it, when, what method/type is it, and one limitation?

This is an individual evidence-reading task, not a substitute for your own oral defence.

Language bridge

English pathway. Deliver the conference in English. You may put a concise German phrase beside an Austrian institution or policy term, then explain its meaning in English. Keep a small bilingual glossary card for terms such as exposure/Betroffenheit, trade-off/Zielkonflikt, and evidence/Beleg. Original-reading extension: return to the assigned short passage from Chartbook 407 (lesson 15) and use one exact idea from it, paraphrased in your own English, to evaluate your case. If a question is unclear, ask for it to be repeated or rephrased; the oral defence assesses subject reasoning separately from fluency.

What to hand in

Final individual or pair investigation, 1,000–1,200 words, with 2–3 charts and at least four sources including independent evidence and a contrasting analysis, as specified in the course project rubric/planner. Each learner also completes a teacher-heard individual oral defence and a 150–200 word reflection. For 24 learners, the 40-minute block provides 100 seconds per learner; larger classes continue in the next lesson. Submit through the teacher’s assignment platform or on paper; the website does not collect work. Success criteria: bounded claim, explained mechanisms, evidence with units/dates, affected groups and power, comparison of responses, uncertainty/limits, and accurate sources.

Exit ticket

What is one question you would keep investigating after this course?

After class · up to 25 minutes

In 15–20 minutes, correct a chart citation or source log after the conference. If already complete, write 100 words explaining which audience question most improved your explanation.
