Sources

Authors, evidence and reading routes

16 double lessons · Austrian grades 10–12
CourseSyllabusGlossaryToolkitProjectSourcesTeacher guide

Read historical sources in their original context. A source’s publication date is not the date of every fact it reports. Classroom readers are original educational summaries, not quotations or endorsed author texts. Hypothetical numbers teach a mechanism; they do not measure a country.

For every source record: author or institution, publication date, claim, evidence, perspective, limitation and URL. Use the source log in the toolkit.

  3. European Commission, EU energy security and gas supplies15 February 2024; checked 6 October 2026 · Russian gas import share 45% in 2021 and 15% in 2023; Commission retrospective framing.
  4. European Commission, Q4 2022 gas and electricity markets17 May 2023; reports Q4 2022 · Quarterly pipeline and LNG flows; not a retail-price measure.
  5. Council of the European Union, gas demand reduction regulation5 August 2022; checked 6 October 2026 · Voluntary 15% demand-reduction target and conditions.
  6. European Commission, Quarterly reports confirm significant recovery in EU gas and electricity markets in Q4 20236 June 2024; reports Q4 2023 · Later market context; optional extension, not used as a current condition.
  7. European Commission, Energy prices and costs in Europe2024 report page; checked 6 October 2026 · Context on wholesale prices rising before the 2022 invasion and falling in 2023; not a claim that one cause explains all movements.

05 · Who should be protected when energy prices rise?

06 · When the world competes for gas

  1. European Commission, Commission makes additional proposals to fight high energy prices and ensure security of supply18 October 2022 · Historical account of proposed joint gas purchasing, solidarity and demand measures; not used to claim a direct effect on third-country prices.
  2. European Commission, Commission report: Emergency energy measures facilitated market improvement5 June 2023 · Context on EU emergency energy measures.
  3. Kate Mackenzie and Tim Sahay, An Introduction (The Polycrisis newsletter)20 October 2022 · Authors’ argument about richer economies bidding for limited gas and poorer importers struggling to afford dollar-priced commodities; paraphrased in under 100 words and labelled as an argument, not proof of a specific causal effect.

07 · Sri Lanka 2022: reconstructing a crisis

  2. IMF, Sri Lanka: Request for an Extended Arrangement Under the Extended Fund Facility—Staff Report20 March 2023 · Retrospective staff analysis of shocks, vulnerabilities, policy choices, reserves, depreciation, debt suspension and the crisis sequence.
  3. IMF, Statement by Krishnamurthy Subramanian, Executive Director for Sri Lanka20 March 2023 · Sri Lankan executive director's perspective and emphasis on policy choices; compared with staff analysis.
  4. World Bank, Unprecedented Shocks Rattle South Asia, Exacerbating Challenges and Dampening Growth6 October 2022 · Contemporaneous account of COVID-19, commodity-price shocks, debt and reserves in South Asia.

08 · Debt, dollars, and room to act
